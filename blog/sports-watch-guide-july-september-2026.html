The Ultimate Sports Watch Guide

Your complete guide to the biggest sporting events from July through September 2026, including MLB, NFL, US Open tennis, Formula 1 and more.

From summer baseball and soccer to the return of football, the US Open, Formula 1, golf's playoff drama and the beginning of hockey season, the next two months are packed with reasons to get together and watch the game.

If you love sports, the calendar is about to get extremely busy.

As we move through the final weeks of July and into August and September, the sports world shifts from the heart of summer into one of the busiest stretches of the year. Baseball is making its push toward the postseason, soccer is in full swing, tennis heads toward the US Open, golf reaches its playoff finale, Formula 1 returns from its summer break, football makes its highly anticipated comeback, and hockey begins to return to the spotlight.

For fans, that means one thing: there is almost always something worth watching.

For a sports bar, it is even better. The next several weeks offer the perfect opportunity to grab your friends, settle in with a cold drink, order some food and enjoy the atmosphere that only live sports can create.

Here is your guide to the biggest sporting action coming up from now through the end of September 2026.

July: Summer Sports Are Still Going Strong

The final days of July are packed with major sporting events, making this an excellent time to get into the rhythm of the busy sports season ahead.

Major League Baseball: The Road to October Begins

Major League Baseball remains one of the biggest attractions throughout the summer, and by late July, every game starts to feel more important.

The MLB season is entering the stretch where contenders begin separating themselves from the rest of the pack. Teams are fighting for division titles, playoff positioning and Wild Card spots, while clubs outside the postseason picture are deciding whether they are buyers or sellers as the trade deadline approaches.

For baseball fans, this is one of the most fascinating times of the year.

The pennant races are beginning to take shape, and every series can have an impact on the postseason picture. A team that goes on a hot streak can suddenly move into contention, while a struggling club can find itself facing difficult decisions about its roster.

At the sports bar, baseball is perfect for a relaxed afternoon or evening. With games spread throughout the day, fans can catch the action while enjoying dinner, drinks and conversation.

And with the regular season continuing into September, the intensity will only increase as summer turns to fall.

Soccer: A Summer of Nonstop Action

Soccer fans have plenty to look forward to throughout the remainder of July and into August.

Major League Soccer continues its regular season, while international soccer and domestic leagues around the world keep the sport on the calendar almost every day.

One of the biggest upcoming events is the 2026 MLS All-Star Game, which is scheduled for July 29 in Charlotte. The event brings together some of the league's biggest stars and adds another major date to an already busy soccer calendar.

For fans who follow European soccer, the end of July and August also represent an exciting transition period. Clubs are preparing for the new season, transfer activity dominates headlines, and preseason matches begin building anticipation for the return of regular league play.

For a sports bar, soccer is one of the best sports for creating an all-day atmosphere. Different leagues and competitions can overlap, giving fans plenty of reasons to stop in, grab a drink and stay for the next match.

Formula 1: The Championship Battle Continues

Formula 1 is also heading into a crucial part of the 2026 season.

The next race on the official F1 calendar is the Hungarian Grand Prix, taking place July 24-26. After that, the championship heads into the summer stretch before returning with the Dutch Grand Prix in August.

The Dutch Grand Prix is scheduled for August 21-23, followed by the Italian Grand Prix from September 4-6, the Spanish Grand Prix from September 11-13 and the Azerbaijan Grand Prix from September 24-26.

That gives racing fans several important weekends to circle on the calendar.

Formula 1 is especially exciting in a sports-bar setting because races create a unique social experience. The combination of speed, strategy, team radio and championship pressure makes every race more than just a sporting event.

August: The Sports Calendar Gets Even Busier

August is where the summer sports calendar really starts to heat up.

Baseball continues its march toward October. Soccer seasons return to full strength. Golf reaches its playoff stage. Tennis fans prepare for the year's final Grand Slam. And football begins to return.

This is arguably one of the best months of the year for sports fans.

Golf: The FedExCup Playoffs Take Center Stage

Golf fans will have plenty to follow as the PGA Tour season moves into its playoff phase.

The FedExCup Playoffs traditionally provide some of the most dramatic golf of the year, as the field becomes smaller and the pressure increases with every round.

By August, players are no longer simply trying to win individual tournaments. They are competing for position, momentum and the opportunity to finish the season as the FedExCup champion.

The playoff atmosphere makes golf especially compelling for fans who enjoy following a tournament over several days. Thursday and Friday rounds build the story, while the weekend brings the pressure of Sunday contention.

For sports bars, golf can also be a great complement to baseball and other live events. Fans can follow the final rounds while enjoying food and drinks, creating a relaxed weekend atmosphere.

The US Open: Tennis Takes Over New York

One of the biggest sporting events of late August and early September is the 2026 US Open.

The tournament is scheduled to run from August 23 through September 13, with main-draw singles play beginning August 30. The event features 22 days of tennis and entertainment, making it one of the most significant sporting spectacles of the summer.

The US Open is the final Grand Slam tournament of the tennis calendar, which means the stakes are enormous.

The world's best players arrive in New York with the opportunity to finish the Grand Slam season on top. Every match brings the possibility of an upset, a breakthrough performance or another chapter in the careers of the sport's biggest stars.

For casual sports fans, the US Open is also an easy event to get into. The matches are fast-paced, dramatic and often unpredictable.

As the tournament moves deeper into the second week, the pressure increases dramatically. The quarterfinals, semifinals and finals create some of the most exciting tennis of the year.

If you enjoy live sports, the US Open is one of those events that deserves a spot on the calendar.

F1 Returns to Europe

Formula 1 returns from its summer break with the Dutch Grand Prix, scheduled for August 21-23.

The return from the break often brings renewed championship drama. Teams have had time to regroup, analyze performance and prepare for the second half of the season.

The Dutch Grand Prix is followed by the legendary Italian Grand Prix at Monza, scheduled for September 4-6.

Monza is one of the most iconic venues in motorsport, and its place on the calendar makes the first weekend of September particularly exciting for racing fans.

Baseball's September Push Begins

By the time August turns into September, MLB fans start paying even closer attention to the standings.

Every series matters more.

Teams that looked like playoff contenders earlier in the season now have to prove they can maintain their position. Clubs battling for Wild Card spots have little room for error, while division leaders try to protect their advantage.

September baseball also brings a unique atmosphere. Younger players may receive opportunities, veterans are trying to make one final push toward October, and every late-inning situation can carry postseason implications.

For sports fans, this is when the phrase "every game matters" starts to feel real.

September: Football Is Back

If there is one event that changes the North American sports landscape, it is the return of football.

The NFL Regular Season Begins

The 2026 NFL regular season begins in September, bringing football fans back together for another full season of Sunday games, Thursday night matchups and Monday night football.

The return of the NFL is always one of the biggest moments on the sports calendar.

Fantasy football leagues are underway. Fans are making predictions. Every team starts the season with hope. The first few weeks are filled with questions that will slowly be answered as teams establish their identities.

Who are the real contenders?

Which young players are ready to break out?

Which teams will surprise everyone?

And which preseason expectations will turn out to be completely wrong?

September football is all about possibilities.

For a sports bar, football season is arguably the most exciting time of the year. Sundays become social events, friends gather to watch multiple games, and fans can follow the entire league throughout the afternoon.

With baseball approaching its postseason and soccer and tennis also delivering major events, September offers one of the best combinations of sports on the entire calendar.

The US Open Reaches Its Conclusion

The US Open continues into September, with the tournament scheduled to conclude on September 13.

By this point, only the best players remain.

The final rounds are where the tournament's biggest stories are written. Players are competing under enormous pressure, and one match can define an entire season.

The US Open final is one of the marquee events in tennis, and the combination of elite athleticism, strategy and emotion makes it a perfect event for sports fans who appreciate high-stakes competition.

Formula 1: Italy, Spain and Azerbaijan

September is a major month for Formula 1.

After the Italian Grand Prix on September 4-6, the championship continues with the Spanish Grand Prix from September 11-13. The season then moves to Azerbaijan for another race weekend from September 24-26.

This portion of the season can be critical for the championship standings.

As the number of remaining races decreases, every point becomes more valuable. Drivers cannot afford mistakes, and teams must balance aggressive racing with championship strategy.

For fans watching at home or at a sports bar, this is where the season can really begin to take shape.

Hockey Starts Coming Back

September also marks the beginning of the transition into hockey season.

The NHL regular season itself traditionally arrives later in the fall, but September is when preseason action, training camps and roster battles start bringing hockey back into the conversation.

For hockey fans, this is an exciting time of year.

New players are trying to earn roster spots. Veterans are preparing for another long season. Teams are evaluating their line combinations and defensive pairings.

The preseason may not carry the same stakes as regular-season hockey, but it signals that another NHL campaign is almost here.

And for fans who have been waiting all summer, that is reason enough to celebrate.

The WNBA Season Heads Toward the Playoffs

Women's basketball will also be a major part of the September sports landscape.

The WNBA season is entering its decisive stages, with teams battling for postseason positioning.

At this point in the season, every game becomes more meaningful. Teams are fighting for playoff spots, while top contenders are looking to secure the best possible position heading into the postseason.

The growing popularity of women's basketball has made the WNBA an increasingly important part of the sports calendar, and September is when the race for the championship becomes especially compelling.

For basketball fans, it is a great opportunity to follow the final stretch of the regular season and see which teams are ready to make a postseason run.

Soccer Keeps the Sports Calendar Full

While football begins to dominate the North American sports conversation, soccer remains a constant throughout September.

MLS continues its regular season, while international leagues return to their normal rhythms following the summer transfer window.

For fans who follow European soccer, September is when the season starts to become more familiar. New signings begin settling into their clubs, teams establish their identities and early-season results begin shaping league tables.

For sports bars, soccer provides an excellent complement to football.

One game can be playing on one screen while another match takes place elsewhere, creating an environment where fans of different sports can all enjoy the action.

The Final Week of September

As September draws to a close, the sports calendar reaches another important transition point.

Baseball enters the final days of its regular season.

Football is several weeks into its campaign.

Soccer leagues are finding their early-season rhythm.

Formula 1 continues its championship fight.

Tennis has crowned a new US Open champion.

And hockey is preparing to return.

The final week of September is essentially the bridge between summer sports and the full fall sports season.

For fans, it is one of the best times of the year.

The Best Sports Months Are Ahead

From now through the end of September, there is no shortage of major sporting action.

Whether you are a football fan waiting for the NFL, a baseball fan following a playoff race, a soccer supporter tracking your club, a tennis fan watching the US Open, a racing enthusiast following Formula 1 or a golf fan keeping an eye on the FedExCup, the next two months offer something for everyone.

And that is what makes this stretch of the sports calendar so special.

The final weeks of summer bring together an incredible variety of sports. Baseball is entering its most meaningful games of the year. Tennis has its final Grand Slam. Golf crowns its season-long champion. Formula 1 returns from its summer break and heads toward the final stages of its championship. Football returns to dominate weekends. Hockey begins preparing for another season. Basketball continues to deliver high-level competition.

For fans, this is the perfect time to make plans with friends, find your favourite seat and enjoy the games together.

Because sports are always better when they are shared.

At CSB Bistro • Sport Bar, the goal is simple: great food, great drinks and a great place to enjoy the action. Whether you're coming in for a big game, catching a race, following the baseball standings or simply looking for somewhere to enjoy the weekend, the upcoming sports calendar gives you plenty of reasons to get together.

So mark your calendar, invite your friends and get ready.

The next two months are going to be busy. And the best part? There is always another game to watch.
